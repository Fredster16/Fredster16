# Phase 3: External Discovery

**Search brief:** look outside the seed set for the gaps found in the DNA map: physical set-ups, product-led images, quality and price proof, mechanisms that work without text, and platform mechanics.

**How to read the scores:**
- **Novelty** is scored against the current database (0 = already in it, 100 = a new mechanism).
- **Transferability** is how well the mechanism fits what your brief says about Deira.
- These are my judgements, not performance data.

---

## D01: Forensic evidence photography

**Source Concept:** crime-scene documentation. Numbered yellow tent markers are placed next to each item so it can be found later in the photos, and L-shaped rulers give scale. Shots are taken with flat, hard flash, either overall or close-up.

**Source Category:** forensic photography / true-crime visual language. I searched for brand campaigns that use evidence markers and found none, so I can't cite an advertising precedent.

**Underlying Mechanism:** genre transplant. Documenting ordinary objects with forensic conventions implies something happened, and the viewer pieces the story together from the items.

**Why it stops attention:** yellow numbered markers read instantly as "incident". Next to harmless items (a chip tray, a wristband), the mismatch between the serious markers and the trivial objects is what grabs.

**Novelty: 90.** No seed uses a physical scene or a borrowed real-world documentation style. The nearest is S09 (a set-up followed by a reveal), but its mechanism and viewer question are different.

**Transferability: 85.** A night-out aftermath fits two subjects in your brief (house music, lad culture). The cap can sit in the scene as an object, the props are cheap, and the forensic look is restrained, not tacky.

**Direct-copy risk:** low. There's no brand execution to copy.

## D02: Anamorphic alignment (Felice Varini)

**Source Concept:** paint fragments spread across walls, floors, railings and ceilings. From one marked spot they line up into a clean geometric shape; from anywhere else they look like random broken marks. Varini picks the vantage point first, projects the shape from it, then traces and paints.

**Source Category:** site-specific public art / installation.

**Underlying Mechanism:** an image that only exists from one viewpoint. The viewer sees chaos first, and a change of position reveals the order.

**Why it stops attention:** slide 1, shot from the wrong angle, shows coloured marks scattered across a real space, and the eye tries and fails to group them. When the aligned shot arrives, the snap from chaos to a perfect shape is the payoff.

**Novelty: 85.** No seed uses a physical space, an optical illusion or a viewpoint reveal. The nearest is S02's decoding, which decodes symbols, not geometry.

**Transferability: 70.** Deira's logo is a simple geometric mark (a large D plus an arrow), which suits tape. It scores lower because it needs a space you're allowed to tape up and a precise set-up.

**Direct-copy risk:** low. Varini's works are abstract shapes, so a logo alignment is a different execution.

## D03: Seamless panorama carousel (Subway)

**Source Concept:** one wide photo cut into equal slices and posted as consecutive carousel slides, so each swipe pans along the same scene. Subway did this with a horizontal photo of a sub, so swiping travelled along the sandwich.

**Source Category:** social-media carousel / ecommerce creative.

**Underlying Mechanism:** the swipe becomes camera movement, and the viewer reveals the scene by swiping.

**Why it stops attention:** slide 1 has an object or person cut off at the right edge, visibly continuing off-frame, so the image is obviously unfinished.

**Novelty: 75.** Seeds use slides as separate frames (S01, S04, S09); none uses the swipe as continuous movement through one image.

**Transferability: 80.** It works on Instagram carousels. On TikTok photo mode each slice has to be cut to 9:16, and I couldn't confirm that TikTok shows slices edge to edge without gaps. **Test this before relying on it.**

**Direct-copy risk:** low. It's a format, not an execution.

## D04: Knolling (Andrew Kromelow / Tom Sachs)

**Source Concept:** objects laid out flat at right angles to each other in a tight grid with equal gaps, shot from directly above. Kromelow named it in 1987 while working as a janitor in Frank Gehry's furniture shop, after the angular Knoll furniture made there. Tom Sachs made it a studio rule ("Always Be Knolling").

**Source Category:** studio practice / product photography.

**Underlying Mechanism:** deconstruction into an ordered grid that shows every part of a thing at once.

**Why it stops attention:** extreme order (right angles, equal gaps) stands out against a feed of messy photos, and a familiar object is shown in pieces.

**Novelty: 80.** No seed shows the product, or any object, taken apart.

**Transferability: 75.** A cap can be unpicked into crown panels, brim, top button, sweatband, back strap, embroidered front panel, label and thread. It costs one cap.

**Direct-copy risk:** low. Knolling is a generic technique.

## D05: Cost transparency (Everlane "Radical Transparency")

**Source Concept:** each product page itemises what the item cost to make (materials, hardware, labour, duties, transport) next to Everlane's price and an estimated traditional retail price.

**Source Category:** ecommerce creative / brand positioning.

**Underlying Mechanism:** expose the cost structure next to the price, so the price justifies itself.

**Why it stops attention:** seeing a product's insides priced line by line is information brands normally hide. As a *visual*, though, Everlane's version is just a table. The visual layer has to come from somewhere else, for example D04 (combined in A03).

**Novelty: 80.** No seed shows a price or proves quality.

**Transferability: 85.** Your customers rank quality first and price second, and your differentiator is price. **But I don't have your unit costs**, and you'd have to decide whether you're willing to publish them.

**Direct-copy risk:** medium. The idea is strongly associated with Everlane, so the visual execution must be your own.

## D06: X-ray of hidden construction (Nick Veasey)

**Source Concept:** industrial X-ray images of garments and objects showing seams, stitches and folds that are normally invisible. Veasey has done this for the V&A's fashion collection, for its Balenciaga exhibition, and for commercial campaigns.

**Source Category:** fine-art photography / museum / advertising.

**Underlying Mechanism:** an unfamiliar imaging method reveals the hidden construction of a familiar object.

**Why it stops attention:** a cap shown as a ghostly, see-through structure doesn't match how caps look, so the eye hunts for the familiar shape.

**Novelty: 85.**

**Transferability: 55.** You can't X-ray. The cheap substitutes are backlighting a single panel on a light source, or a clip-on macro lens on stitching, and I haven't tested how much cotton twill shows when backlit. The substitutes are weaker than the source. **Not adapted this round.**

**Direct-copy risk:** low.

## D07: Shadow sculpture (Tim Noble & Sue Webster)

**Source Concept:** a heap of rubbish lit by a single projector casts a shadow on the wall that's a precise portrait of the artists (e.g. *Dirty White Trash (with Gulls)*, 1998).

**Source Category:** contemporary art installation.

**Underlying Mechanism:** the object looks meaningless but its shadow is the image. One set-up gives two readings.

**Why it stops attention:** the mismatch between a heap of junk and a crisp silhouette behind it.

**Novelty: 85.**

**Transferability: 45.** The Deira version would be a pile of night-out debris (cans, glowsticks, wristbands) throwing the D-arrow logo as a shadow. That's a strong idea, but building a pile that casts an accurate shape takes long trial and error. Difficulty is high for one post. **Not adapted this round.**

**Direct-copy risk:** low.

---

## D08: Odd-one-out search (Gergely Dudás, "find the panda", 2015)

**Source Concept:** a dense cartoon grid of near-identical snowmen with one panda hidden among them. It was shared 145,000+ times on Facebook, and Dudás followed it with a cat hidden among owls.

**Source Category:** puzzle illustration / viral image.

**Underlying Mechanism:** a visual search task with a clear win condition.

**Why it stops attention:** a dense repeating pattern plus an implied challenge. The viewer stays on the image until they find the odd one out.

**Novelty: 45.** It overlaps with S07: both make the viewer search the image for something hidden. S07 hides text by *size*; this hides an object by *camouflage*.

**Transferability: 70.** The grey colourway can be hidden in a real grey scene. **Not adapted this round** because of the overlap with S07; held as a candidate for the grey-colourway push.

**Direct-copy risk:** low.

## Reviewed, not added to the database

**Fake out-of-home / CGI giant product** (Jacquemus' Le Bambino bags on wheels through Paris, April 2023; Maybelline's mascara train). The mechanism is impossible scale in a real location. **Transferability: 35.** Good CGI costs well beyond your current revenue, your brief says "never tacky", and cheap CGI or AI-generated versions are where this format looks tacky. The cheap practical alternative, forced perspective, is a common tourist photo with little novelty.
