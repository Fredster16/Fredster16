## S01: Rock beats scissors

**Images:** 2, then 1 (slide order). Deira-branded: the labels say "buy Deira Clo".

**Raw Hook:** "image 1+2: image 2 first / then image 1 / the product images"

**What appears visually:**
- *Slide 1 (image 2):* a plain white portrait canvas. Two real, cut-out hand photos meet just below the vertical centre.
  - **Left hand:** enters from the left edge as a clenched fist (rock), knuckles facing right. It has a black ribbed-knit sleeve with a frayed cuff and two silver bracelets: a thin chain and a chunky curb chain. A soft **green** glow surrounds the fist.
  - **Right hand:** enters from the right edge, with index and middle fingers extended flat and pointing at the fist (scissors). A silver cross-motif ring sits on the middle finger, and a soft **red** glow surrounds the hand.
  - **Labels:** two lines of heavy black lowercase sans-serif, centred above each hand. Left (fist): "save money". Right (scissors): "buy other brands".
  - No product and no logo.
- *Slide 2 (image 1):* the identical photo and layout. Only the labels change: the fist (green) reads "buy Deira Clo" and the scissors (red) reads "save money".
- *Slides 3+:* product images (not supplied).

**First focal point:** the green and red glows around the two hands. They are the only saturated colour on a white frame, at the centre where the knuckles and fingertips nearly touch. The labels are read immediately after.

**Visual mechanism:** a ranking based on a rule everyone knows. Rock-paper-scissors (rock beats scissors), backed up by the green = win / red = lose glows, ranks two labelled options with no explanation. Across two slides it becomes a **chained ranking**: slide 1 sets A > B, then slide 2 puts a new option above A.

**Attention trigger:** two hands squaring up on an otherwise empty white frame, colour-coded like traffic lights. The frame contains nothing else for the eye to go to.

**Curiosity mechanism:** weak to moderate. Slide 1 is a complete thought ("saving money beats buying other brands"). The only pull to swipe is the carousel indicator and the expectation that a "versus" meme escalates. What's held back is what could beat "save money".

**Information gap:** "Is there anything that beats saving money?"

**Visual payoff:** slide 2 keeps the same hands and promotes "buy Deira Clo" to the winning green fist, while "save money" drops to the losing red scissors.

**Production method:**
1. Photograph both hand poses against a plain light wall in soft daylight, from the same angle with the hands level.
2. Remove the backgrounds using any background remover (Photoshop, Canva, or iOS long-press "lift subject").
3. Place the hands on a white canvas: 1080×1350 for Instagram, or 1080×1920 for TikTok.
4. Add a wide, soft outer glow: saturated green behind the fist and saturated red behind the scissors hand.
5. Set the labels in a heavy grotesque sans, lowercase and centred.
6. Duplicate the file and swap the labels to make slide 2.

**Required assets:** one person's hands (both poses), a dark sleeve, bracelets and ring (styling only), an editing app with background removal, glow and text, and product photos for slides 3+.

**Difficulty:** 2/10

**Estimated production requirements:** physical setup: minimal · photography: yes (2 shots) · video frame: no · Photoshop/image generation: background removal + glow · graphic design: yes · location: no · actors/models: hands only · props: jewellery (optional)

**Reusable principle:** use a rule system the whole audience already knows (a game, a league table, a food chain) so a ranking reads instantly. Open with a ranking the viewer agrees with, then put something new on top in the next slide.

**Risk of becoming repetitive:** medium. You can re-label the hand pair a few times, but after 3–4 uses the audience will predict the layout. The underlying principle has plenty of other carriers (Top Trumps cards, league tables, food chains).

**Weaknesses:**
- Slide 1 carries no Deira cue, so a viewer who stops but doesn't swipe gives Deira nothing.
- Slide 2 says "Deira is worth spending on", not "Deira is the better price". Your brief makes price the differentiator, but price never appears, so the post can read as "Deira is the splurge".
- The meaning lives entirely in the text. Remove the labels and the image says nothing.

## S02: Greyscale vs rainbow

**Image:** 3 (screenshot of another account's post)

**Raw Hook:** "its a grey colourway but it also leaves intrigue"

**What appears visually:** a white canvas, roughly square. Three full-body black silhouettes walk along a thin ground line in the lower two-thirds. They're drawn in a flat, AI-illustration style, with white contour lines for clothing details.
- **Left:** a woman with long wavy hair walking left, away from the others. She wears an oversized sweatshirt, baggy cargo trousers, a small shoulder bag and chunky trainers.
- **Centre:** a young man in an oversized zip hoodie, chain, baggy trousers and Nike trainers. He walks right but turns his head back and down towards the left woman.
- **Right:** a woman in a crop top and cargo trousers holding his hand. She looks up at him, with a cartoon anger symbol (💢) by her head.
- **Swatches:** a small rounded rectangle above each woman. Over the left woman it's six bands running from grey to black; over the right woman, rainbow bands.
- There are no words. Structurally this is the "distracted boyfriend" set-up.

**First focal point:** the rainbow swatch at top right, the only colour in a black-and-white image. The eye then jumps to the matching grey swatch at the same height on the left, and only then down to the figures.

**Visual mechanism:** symbolic colour coding plus a familiar relationship template. Each woman's style is reduced to a palette chip over her head. The man's head-turn tells you which one he prefers. It's a wordless visual riddle.

**Attention trigger:** one patch of saturated colour on a monochrome frame, plus body language everyone recognises: his head turned away from his partner, and her anger symbol.

**Curiosity mechanism:** the meaning of the swatches is never stated. The viewer has to work out that the palettes describe how each woman dresses, and that the lad, dressed in black, prefers the monochrome girl.

**Information gap:** "What do the colour bars mean?" Once that's solved: "Why does he want the grey one?"

**Visual payoff:** later slides weren't supplied. Per your note, the payoff is the grey colourway: grey "wins".

**Production method:** generate the figures with an image generator; the line style and identical silhouettes look AI-generated. Alternatively, draw them as vector illustration. Draw the swatches as rectangles in any design app and compose on white.

**Required assets:** an image generator or illustrator, two swatch graphics, and a grey-cap product shot for the payoff.

**Difficulty:** 3/10 with an image generator, 6/10 if hand-illustrated.

**Estimated production requirements:** physical setup: no · photography: no (payoff only) · video frame: no · Photoshop/image generation: yes · graphic design: yes · location: no · actors/models: no · props: no

**Reusable principle:** replace each character's description with one abstract symbol above their head, and stage a relationship everyone recognises. The viewer decodes the symbols, the decoding is the reward, and the product is the answer to the code.

**Risk of becoming repetitive:** high. Four of your 10 seeds (S02, S03, S05, and the crowd in S08) use this same black-silhouette AI style, which suggests it's already common in the feeds you're collecting from. The "lads only wear black" joke also has only one punchline.

**Weakness:** the decoding effort is high. With no words, a viewer who doesn't crack the swatches within a second or two scrolls on. On its own the image promotes nothing; the grey-cap link only lands on the next slide.

**Curiosity mechanism:** NONE held back. The pull is a scanning task ("how many of these did I have?") rather than a question that needs a swipe.

The alternative is to fill the 2026 body with current-life items from your brief (house music, football) and put the Deira cap on the head, where the original already has a cap.

**What appears visually:** a candid portrait photo in a hotel corridor, with "SUITES 610-619 / 620-629" wall signs and a green EXIT sign.
- **Centre foreground:** a smiling woman walking towards the camera in a black cropped top and light-blue straight-leg jeans, labelled "*Skinny Jeans" in white across her legs.

**First focal point:** the smiling woman in the centre: largest, frontmost, and the brightest area (skin tones and blue denim). The man flattened against the wall is noticed second.

**Visual mechanism:** a labelled candid photo (the "exploitable image" meme). Real people's body language gets relabelled as abstract things; here, flattening against a wall = avoiding something.

**Attention trigger:** a group that looks famous in a real setting, plus one man's odd posture.

**Curiosity mechanism:** NONE. The joke is complete once both labels are read.

**Information gap:** none. The reaction is identification ("same").

**Visual payoff:** immediate, on the same frame.

**Production method:** one source photo plus two text labels.

**Required assets:** the photo and text.

**Difficulty:** 1/10

**Estimated production requirements:** physical setup: no · photography: no (found photo) · video frame: no · Photoshop/image generation: no · graphic design: text only · location: no · actors/models: no · props: no

**Reusable principle:** find or stage a photo where someone's body language already expresses a feeling (avoiding, longing, dodging), then label the people as the things your audience feels that way about.

**Risk of becoming repetitive:** high. Each post needs a fresh photo with the right body language, and the label format is everywhere.

**Notes on your Deira version** ("*Me" avoiding "*expensive low quality caps"):
- **The mapping works.** Pressing against the wall maps cleanly onto refusing overpriced caps.
- **This specific photo is a risk for a brand account.**
  - *Copyright:* the photo belongs to whoever took it, and using it in a post that sells caps is commercial use without a licence.
  - *Implied endorsement:* the UK has no general image right, but selling something in a way that implies a famous person endorsed it can be passing off. See *Fenty v Arcadia* (Rihanna v Topshop), [2015] EWCA Civ 3.
- **Safer version:** restage the same body language yourselves, with you, your cousin and mates in a corridor or club entrance, and label it. That also puts the owners in front of the audience, and your brief lists direct contact with the owners as a reason people buy.

## S07: Girl, Zoom In

**Image:** 9 (screenshot of another account's post)

**Raw Hook:** "mens hand 'mate zoom in' 'deira just dropped the grey colourway'"

**What appears visually:** a white portrait canvas.
- **Headline:** heavy condensed black type, "Girl, Zoom In", in the upper-middle.
- **Hand:** below left, a vintage-print hand (halftone texture, brown skin, long almond nails, a gold nugget ring on the index finger) enters from the left edge. It makes a "this much" gesture: index finger and thumb held horizontal and parallel with a gap between them.
- **Hidden text:** in the gap, on a faint white blur, a line of tiny text, "you need more clothes", at roughly 1% of the image height.

**First focal point:** "Girl, Zoom In", the largest and heaviest element.

**Visual mechanism:** a direct instruction to physically interact (pinch-zoom), plus text too small to read at normal size. The "tiny amount" gesture frames and points at that text.

**Attention trigger:** a command addressed to the viewer ("Girl"), surrounded by mostly empty white space.

**Curiosity mechanism:** the message is held back by its size, and the viewer has to zoom to read it.

**Information gap:** "What does the tiny text say?"

**Visual payoff:** "you need more clothes", a cheeky call-out of the viewer. Because it's a joke at their expense, it rewards the effort of zooming. It's also the sales line for a clothing account.

**Production method:** a hand image (stock or own photo), the headline, and micro-text at about 10–12 px on a 1080-px-wide canvas.

**Required assets:** a hand image and fonts.

**Difficulty:** 1/10

**Estimated production requirements:** physical setup: no · photography: optional (own hand) · video frame: no · Photoshop/image generation: no · graphic design: yes · location: no · actors/models: hand only · props: ring (styling)

**Reusable principle:** hide the real message at a size that can only be read by zooming, tell the viewer to zoom, and make what they find worth the effort.

**Notes on your Deira version** (man's hand, "mate zoom in", hidden "deira just dropped the grey colourway"):
