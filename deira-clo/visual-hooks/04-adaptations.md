## A01: Exhibit 7

**Source → mechanism:** forensic evidence photography (D01) → documenting ordinary items as evidence implies a story. The source-specific elements (crime, police) are removed and replaced with the morning after a house night.

**Core Mechanism:** the morning after a night out is photographed like a crime scene, with yellow numbered markers next to the debris and the Deira cap as the last exhibit.

**Slide 1 Visual:**
- **Orientation / crop:** portrait. Shoot 9:16 and crop to 4:5 for Instagram.
- **Camera angle:** standing height, looking down at about 45° onto a plain wooden kitchen table. The on-camera flash is ON for hard shadows and flat colour, which is the forensic look. No filter.
- **Subject:** the tabletop, filling the frame edge to edge. The table's far edge is just inside the top of the frame.
- **Props:** six items, each with a yellow numbered tent marker just in front and to its left:
  1. A paper event wristband, torn open
  2. A polystyrene chip tray, half-eaten, with a wooden fork
  3. A crumpled taxi receipt
  4. A used glowstick
  5. An empty can on its side
  6. A lanyard
- **Product:** marker 7, bottom-right foreground: the grey Deira cap, front panel and logo facing the lens, sitting upright and clean. It's the only undamaged object in the scene.
- **Foreground:** marker 7 and the cap, the largest object in frame because it's nearest the lens.
- **Background:** the table's far edge, with nothing beyond it in focus.
- **Relative scale:** the markers are about can height, the cap is about twice the footprint of any other item, and there's roughly 15 cm between items.
- **Visual hierarchy:** the yellow markers (the most saturated colour) come first, then the numbers 1→7, then the items, ending on the cap at 7.
- **Text:** none, or a single small timestamp in plain white sans at the top. Leave copy to you.

**What the viewer notices first:** the yellow numbered markers.

**Immediate viewer question:** "What happened here?"

**Slide 2 Direction:** one close-up per marker, in order (slides 2–7). Each is shot from directly above with the same flash, with the marker in frame next to its item, filling the frame. Each one adds a piece of the night, and the order walks the viewer through it.

**Payoff Direction:** the final slide is marker 7. It's a flash close-up of the cap's front panel and stitching, sharp and clean: the only thing that came through the night intact. The punchline is a quality claim made without saying "quality".

**Why this works for Deira specifically:**
- House music is your long-term link and both owners' passion, and a night out is the scene.
- Your customers' interests include classic British lad culture: the chip tray, the taxi receipt.
- Quality is their #1 buying priority, and the cap surviving the night is the payoff.
- "Never tacky": the forensic look is restrained.
- Your photography style includes studio and street, and this is real photography of a real object.

**Assets Required:**
- A grey Deira cap
- 7 yellow numbered tent markers (printed on card and folded, or a bought plastic set)
- A paper event wristband
- A chip tray and wooden fork
- A taxi receipt
- A glowstick
- An empty can
- A lanyard
- A wooden table
- A camera or phone with flash
- Optional: a printed L-shaped scale ruler

**Production Method:**
1. Lay out the items with about 15 cm gaps and place each marker in front and to the left of its item.
2. Shoot the overall frame at 45° with flash.
3. Shoot 7 close-ups from above with flash, each including its marker.
4. Use one white balance throughout. In editing, only raise contrast slightly.
5. Order the slides 1 → 7.

If the wristband shows a real event's name, you're visibly tagging that event, so use one from a night you actually went to.

**Difficulty:** 3/10 · **Cost Level:** £1–£10 (printed markers; props from a night out)

**Scores:** Originality 80 · Stop-Power 70 · Brand Relevance 80 · Execution Simplicity 85 · **Overall: pending formula**

**Database Similarity: 15/100** (closest: S09, a set-up then a reveal; different mechanism, viewer question and set-up)

## A02: Front of the Queue

**Source → mechanism:** Subway's panorama carousel (D03) → the swipe becomes camera movement through one scene. The sandwich is removed and replaced with a club queue and your tagline.

**Core Mechanism:** one queue photo split across five seamless slides, so swiping walks the viewer up the line to the front, where the first person is in the Deira cap: "Built for those who move first".

**Slide 1 Visual:**
- **Orientation / crop:** one stitched panorama, 5 × 1080 px wide by 1350 px tall for Instagram, sliced into five 4:5 slides.
- **Camera angle:** tripod at chest height, parallel to a brick wall about 3 m away, eye-level and flat-on. Night, lit by a streetlamp and the venue door light.
- **Subject (slide 1):** the *back* of a pavement queue along the wall. Three people stand side-on to camera in hoods and other caps, looking at their phones. The fourth person is cut in half by the right edge of the frame, so the queue visibly continues off-frame.
- **Product:** none on slide 1.
- **Foreground:** pavement kerb along the bottom 10% of the frame.
- **Background:** the brick wall, filling the frame behind the people.
- **Relative scale:** people at full height, heads in the top third.
- **Visual hierarchy:** the person cut off at the right edge, then the line of heads leading right.

**What the viewer notices first:** the queue running off the right edge.

**Immediate viewer question:** "What's this queue for, and who's at the front?"

**Slide 2 Direction:** slides 2–4 continue the queue seamlessly, one person cut by each edge so the scene reads as continuous. Small, human details along the way: someone holding a cap in their hand, two lads sharing a phone, someone checking the time.

**Payoff Direction:** slide 5 shows the venue door, the bouncer, and the first person in line in the grey Deira cap, turned towards the camera. It's the only face in the panorama looking at the lens. Optional slide 6: a close-up of the cap.

**Why this works for Deira specifically:**
- Your tagline, "Built for those who move first", becomes a literal image: first in the queue.
- A club queue is a house-music image (your long-term link).
- The queue can be cast from your own followers and mates, which makes the tight-knit community the cast.
- Your listed photography style includes street.

**Assets Required:**
- 10–15 people
- A brick wall or entrance where you have permission to shoot (a venue's door if you have access; I don't know whether you do)
- A tripod and camera
- One grey Deira cap (more if extras wear them)
- Stitching software (Photoshop Photomerge, or free Hugin)
- A slicing tool

**Production Method:**
1. Shoot five overlapping frames (about 30% overlap) from a tripod, shifting it sideways along the wall, *not* rotating it. Everyone holds still.
2. Stitch the frames, crop to 5400 × 1350, and slice into five 1080 × 1350 images.
3. Upload them in order.
4. For TikTok, re-slice at 9:16 and test one post privately first. I couldn't confirm that TikTok's photo mode shows the slices edge to edge.

**Difficulty:** 6/10 · **Cost Level:** £0 if people volunteer. Your access to 10–15 people and a location is unknown.

**Scores:** Originality 70 · Stop-Power 55 (slide 1 is a calm image, and its pull relies on the cut-off edge) · Brand Relevance 90 · Execution Simplicity 45 · **Overall: pending formula**

**Database Similarity: 30/100** (closest: S08, many people in a line. Similar surface, but a different mechanism: a swipe pan vs a fork comparison)

## A03: £27.99, Unpicked

**Source → mechanism:** **combination** of knolling (D04) and cost transparency (D05).
- Knolling alone is a striking but empty image.
- Cost transparency alone is a table, not a visual.
- Together, the grid gives the cost breakdown something to point at, and the cost breakdown gives the grid a reason to exist.

**Core Mechanism:** a Deira cap unpicked into every component and laid out in a right-angled grid, with each part then priced so the sum explains the price.

**Slide 1 Visual:**
- **Orientation / crop:** portrait 4:5.
- **Camera angle:** directly overhead at 90°, lens parallel to the surface, on a plain mid-grey paper sweep.
- **Lighting:** two soft, even light sources from the sides (or window light plus white card), with no hard shadows.
- **Subject:** the parts of one Deira cap in a grid (3 columns × 4 rows), all edges at right angles to each other, with equal ~2 cm gaps:
  - 5 plain crown panels
  - The embroidered front panel with the D-arrow logo
  - Brim
  - Top button
  - Sweatband
  - Back strap / closure
  - Inner label
  - Thread
- **Product:** the embroidered front panel, placed in the centre cell. It's the largest and the only part carrying the logo.
- **Foreground / background:** flat; the grey sweep fills the frame.
- **Relative scale:** the brim and front panel are the largest pieces; the button and thread are smallest, in the corners.
- **Visual hierarchy:** the logo panel in the centre first, then the grid order, then the small parts.
- **Text on slide 1:** none.

**What the viewer notices first:** the embroidered D-arrow panel in the centre of a grid of flat pieces.

**Immediate viewer question:** "Is that a cap cut into pieces? What's actually in one?"

**Slide 2 Direction:** the identical frame, with a small printed tag next to each part showing what it costs you.

**Payoff Direction:**
- **Slide 3:** the total cost to make vs your price, with your margin shown honestly.
- **Slide 4:** the whole cap, assembled, shot from the same overhead angle.
- **The payoff:** the price is explained by what's in it.

Whether you compare against other brands' prices is your call. Naming competitors is a business decision I won't make for you.

**Why this works for Deira specifically:**
- Quality and price are your customers' #1 and #2 buying priorities.
- Price is your differentiator.
- Direct contact with the owners is part of "why us", and opening the books is that, made visual.
- "Studio" is in your listed photography style.

**Assets Required:**
- **One Deira cap to sacrifice** (unit cost unknown to me)
- A seam ripper
- Mid-grey paper backdrop
- An overhead mount or tripod with a horizontal arm
- 2 soft lights or window light with white card
- Printed price tags
- **Your real per-component costs** (not provided)

**Production Method:**
1. Unpick the cap along its seams with the seam ripper, keeping every piece intact.
2. Press the panels flat.
3. Lay out the grid with a ruler.
4. Shoot slide 1 from overhead.
5. Add the tags without moving the camera, and shoot slide 2.
6. Shoot the whole cap from the same angle.

**Difficulty:** 5/10 · **Cost Level:** £1–£10 plus one cap's unit cost

**Scores:** Originality 65 (both parent techniques exist; the combination is less common) · Stop-Power 60 · Brand Relevance 90 · Execution Simplicity 60 · **Overall: pending formula**

**Database Similarity: 10/100** (no seed shows the product, a price, or deconstruction)

**Blocked on:** your cost data, and your decision on whether to publish your margin.

## A04: Line It Up

**Source → mechanism:** Felice Varini (D02) → an image that only exists from one viewpoint. The abstract geometry is removed and replaced with the D-arrow logo in tape, across a space with sound-system props.

**Core Mechanism:** white tape fragments across a room only form the Deira logo from one exact spot. Slide 1 is the wrong angle, and the last slide is the alignment.

**Slide 1 Visual:**
- **Orientation / crop:** portrait 4:5 or 9:16.
- **Location:** a room you control, e.g. a garage with a grey concrete floor and brick or block walls.
- **Camera angle:** eye level, standing about 90° to the side of the alignment point.
- **Subject:** strips of 48 mm white tape running across:
  - The floor
  - The back wall
  - A side wall
  - A speaker box and a chair standing in the room
  
  From this angle the strips are broken, curved fragments at random-looking angles.
- **Product:** none on slide 1.
- **Foreground:** the floor strips, running out of the bottom of the frame.
- **Background:** the back wall, with tape fragments.
- **Relative scale:** the logo spans the whole room, about 3 m across when aligned.
- **Visual hierarchy:** bright white strips on a dark room, then the speaker box (the only object).

**What the viewer notices first:** bright white tape strips scattered over floor, walls and a speaker box at odd angles.

**Immediate viewer question:** "What's this meant to be?"

**Slide 2 Direction:** the camera moves halfway towards the vantage point. Some strips start to join into a curve and others still don't.

**Payoff Direction:**
- **Slide 3:** from the vantage point, every strip snaps into the D-arrow logo filling the frame, edges continuous across floor, wall and speaker.
- **Slide 4:** a person in the grey cap standing at the edge of the room, outside the logo's lines, or a close-up of the embroidered logo shot at the same angle.

**Why this works for Deira specifically:**
- Your brief names the logo as your "signature", and the logo is a simple geometric mark, which is what this technique needs.
- It works with no text, filling the 1/10 gap in the database.
- The speaker box ties it to the house-music link.

**Assets Required:**
- A room you control
- 2–3 rolls of 48 mm white tape
- A tripod and camera
- **Either** a laptop running free OBS with the camera feed and a semi-transparent logo overlay, **or** a mini projector
- 2 people
- A speaker box and a chair
- The grey cap
- Your logo as a PNG

**Production Method:**
1. Fix the tripod at the vantage point.
2. Either show the live camera feed on a laptop with the logo PNG overlaid at 50% opacity, or project the logo from the camera position.
3. One person watches the overlay and directs; the other lays tape along the logo's lines on every surface.
4. Shoot from the vantage point.
5. Shoot from halfway and from 90° to the side.
6. Order the slides: wrong angle → halfway → aligned.

**Difficulty:** 7/10 · **Cost Level:** £1–£10 (tape) using the laptop method, or £26–£50 if you buy a projector

**Scores:** Originality 80 · Stop-Power 70 · Brand Relevance 70 · Execution Simplicity 35 · **Overall: pending formula**

**Database Similarity: 20/100** (closest: S02, a decoding reward; but S02 decodes symbols and this resolves geometry through movement)

## A05: Cupid's D (seed variation, flagged)

**Source:** seed S04 (and S10). **This is a variation of an existing hook, not a new mechanism.** It's included because it fixes S04's main weakness: the format is already used by other brands, and Deira's logo is the one completion of it that only Deira can do.

**Core Mechanism:** in a fake chat, the girlfriend draws an arrow and asks "complete it? 🥺", expecting Cupid's heart. The reply draws the D around the arrow, which is the actual Deira logo.

**Slide 1 Visual:**
- **Orientation / crop:** portrait, a full-screen black iMessage thread.
- **Layout:**
  - Right-aligned blue bubble: "Babe".
  - Below it, a white image card at about 70% width, holding one hand-drawn black arrow (shaft, arrowhead, fletching) crossing the card at the same angle the arrow crosses the D in your logo.
  - Blue bubble: "complete it? 🥺".
  - Read receipt.
  - Grey typing indicator on the left.
- **Product:** none on slide 1.
- **Visual hierarchy:** the white card with the lone arrow, then the "complete it?" bubble, then the typing dots.
- **Lower half:** empty black.

**What the viewer notices first:** a lone arrow on a white card.

**Immediate viewer question:** "What's he going to draw around the arrow?" The viewer expects a heart.

**Slide 2 Direction:** the grey reply plus a white card with a large hand-drawn **D** drawn around the arrow, matching your logo's geometry. Then her reply, which is copy for you to write in your own voice.

**Payoff Direction:** slide 3 is a product shot of the embroidered D-arrow on the cap's front panel, shot at the same angle as the drawing so the two match.

**Why this works for Deira specifically:** your brief describes the logo as a large "D" with an arrow through it, which is the same structure as a heart pierced by Cupid's arrow. In S04, the brand name only loosely completes the heart. Here the completion *is* the logo, so the joke can't be reused by another brand.

**Assets Required:**
- Two phones (or a chat mock-up template)
- Two drawings (the arrow; the D around the arrow)
- Your logo for reference
- A product close-up

**Production Method:**
1. Run a real thread between two phones, using the iMessage drawing or an image of the drawing.
2. Screenshot at the typing-indicator stage, then again after the reply.

**Difficulty:** 2/10 · **Cost Level:** £0

**Scores:** Originality 40 (a variation on a circulating meme) · Stop-Power 65 · Brand Relevance 85 · Execution Simplicity 95 · **Overall: pending formula**

**Database Similarity: 75/100 to S04** and 70/100 to S10: same mechanism and set-up, with a different completion. **Flagged**, not auto-rejected, because no threshold is set.
